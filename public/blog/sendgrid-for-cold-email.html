Cold email

Can you use SendGrid for cold email? Short answer: no

People ask this because SendGrid is cheap, has an API, and gets mail out the door. But cold email from SendGrid fails for three reasons that have nothing to do with your copy.

Reason one: the shared IP pool

SendGrid sends from shared IPs by default. Those IPs carry transactional and marketing mail from many customers, and they are well known to mailbox providers. Cold outreach is the most filter-sensitive kind of mail: unknown sender, no prior relationship, high volume per recipient segment. Mail from a well-known shared pool with cold-email patterns is exactly what spam filters are tuned to catch. Your mail does not need to be bad; it needs to be different, and a shared SendGrid IP makes it look like everyone else.

Reason two: the terms of service

SendGrid's acceptable use policy restricts unsolicited bulk email. Cold email at volume is unsolicited bulk email. The risk is not just deliverability; it is the account. A provider can throttle or suspend a sending account that violates its policy, and your outreach pipeline stops the day it matters.

Reason three: your brand domain

Most teams send cold email from their main domain or a subdomain of it. If the sending reputation of that domain sinks, your transactional mail, your receipts, and your support mail follow it into the spam folder. Cold email is a reputation gamble, and you should never run the gamble on the domain your business runs on.

What actually works

A dedicated sending domain. A cheap second domain, nothing else on it. Your brand domain stays clean for transactional mail.

A provider that allows it. Either a cold-email tool built for outreach, or a transactional provider where cold outreach is explicitly permitted, with a dedicated IP.

Proper DNS on the sending domain. SPF authorizing only the provider you use, DKIM signing, DMARC in place, and a working MX or a clear explanation of why the domain does not receive mail.

Warmup and low volume. Two to four weeks of ramping, starting at 10 to 20 emails a day, growing slowly. No provider setting fixes a cold IP that suddenly sends 500 emails a day.

Plain text, one link, a real address. Cold email that looks like marketing gets treated like marketing.

Whichever provider you pick, the setup on your domain decides the outcome.
