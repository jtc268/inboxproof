SendGrid vs Postmark: which transactional email provider actually delivers?

SendGrid and Postmark are both workhorses for transactional email, and both will get your mail out the door. The difference is what happens after that: where your mail comes from, who shares that reputation with you, and what the provider will let you send. SendGrid is built for volume and flexibility. Postmark is built for one thing, getting transactional mail into the inbox, and it makes real trade-offs to do that.

The core difference: how IPs work

SendGrid sends from a shared IP pool by default. Your mail leaves from IPs that many other customers also use, which means their sending behavior affects your reputation. If a neighbor on your IP starts sending spam or gets a bad bounce rate, your mail can suffer. You can move to a dedicated IP on higher tiers, but a new dedicated IP starts with no reputation and needs weeks of careful warmup before it is safe for real volume.

Postmark takes the opposite approach. It sends from a pool of IPs that it manages with a strict reputation policy: transactional mail only, no marketing campaigns, no shared sending with bulk senders. In practice that means if your Postmark mail lands in spam, the cause is almost always your list, your content, or your authentication setup, not a noisy neighbor. Postmark will also tell you plainly when something is wrong instead of letting you burn reputation quietly.

What each is good at

SendGrid is the better fit when you need more than transactional mail: marketing campaigns, templates, A/B testing, big volume at a lower price per email, and a large ecosystem of integrations and SDKs. The free tier is a real on-ramp for small projects.

Postmark is the better fit when transactional mail is the whole job: password resets, order confirmations, account alerts, receipts. Its streams feature lets you separate mail types (for example, transactional and internal test mail) so one stream never drags down the other. It does not do marketing campaigns at all, which is a feature for deliverability and a deal-breaker if you need them.

Developer experience

Both are API-first with SMTP access and solid documentation. SendGrid has more SDKs, more integrations, and more knobs. Postmark has a smaller, cleaner API and fewer ways to misconfigure things. For a team that sends one or two kinds of mail and wants it to arrive, Postmark is the lower-maintenance choice. For a platform that sends many kinds of mail and needs campaigns, SendGrid is the more capable one.

Pricing

SendGrid prices by monthly volume with a free tier, and the price per email drops as you scale. Postmark prices per 10,000 emails with no free tier, so it costs more at low volume and buys you the reputation model above. Both change their pricing, so check the current numbers before you commit.

The verdict

Pick SendGrid if you send marketing as well as transactional, need high volume at low cost, or want the bigger ecosystem. Pick Postmark if you only send transactional mail and deliverability is the entire job. And with either one, the SPF, DKIM and DMARC setup on your domain decides most of the outcome.
