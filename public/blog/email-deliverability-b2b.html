Email deliverability for B2B: what works when you are sending to business inboxes

B2B email deliverability is different from B2C. Business inboxes have stricter filters, higher bounce rates, and different engagement patterns. If you are sending to business addresses, you need to understand how the major mailbox providers treat B2B email differently, and what to do about it.

Why B2B deliverability is harder

Business inboxes are managed by IT departments, not individuals. That means the filters are stricter, the bounce rates are higher, and the engagement patterns are different. A business inbox is more likely to reject an email based on the sender's domain reputation, the email's authentication, and the content's relevance.

Business inboxes also have higher bounce rates. Employees change jobs, companies change domains, and mailboxes get deleted. If you are sending to a list of business addresses that is six months old, you are probably sending to a lot of dead addresses. That is a fast way to tank your sender reputation.

What to check for B2B deliverability

Start with the basics. Your SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records must be clean. Business inboxes are more likely to reject an email that fails authentication than consumer inboxes are. Run the free DMARC checker on your sending domain and make sure all three records are in place and passing.

Next, check your domain reputation. Business inboxes are more likely to check your domain's reputation before deciding where to deliver your email. If your domain has a history of sending spam, your B2B email will go to the spam folder no matter how good the content is.

Then, check your bounce rate. B2B lists have higher bounce rates than B2C lists. If your bounce rate is above two percent, you need to clean your list before you send. A high bounce rate is one of the fastest ways to get your domain blacklisted.

How to keep your B2B email out of spam

Keep your list clean. Verify every address before you send. Remove the dead addresses, the typo addresses, and the inactive subscribers. A clean list is the foundation of every other deliverability fix.

Warm up your IP slowly. If you are sending from a new IP, start with a low volume and increase it gradually over a few weeks. A sudden spike in volume is a red flag for mailbox providers.

Monitor your sender reputation. Use a tool like Inboxproof Pro to track your domain's reputation and get alerts when something breaks. B2B inboxes are more likely to check your reputation, so you need to know when it changes.

When B2B deliverability is the actual problem

If your authentication is clean, your domain reputation is good, and your content is solid, but your open rates are still low and your bounce rate is still high, the problem is probably your list. Run the free audit on your sending domain and look at the bounce rate. If it is above two percent, that is your first project, not the subject line.

Work it in this order: clean the list, fix the authentication, then optimize the content. Most teams do it backwards, which is why they burn weeks on copy while the real problem sits underneath. Once the list is clean, the rest of the deliverability work you already know how to do will actually pay off.
