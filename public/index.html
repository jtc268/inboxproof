What we check

See what needs attention

Review public DNS records and accessible mail-server settings. These checks identify configuration issues; they do not test inbox placement.

MX & mail routing

Look up MX records and check whether the selected mail host resolves to an IP address.

SPF

Inspect the published SPF record, its top-level lookup mechanisms, and its policy for unlisted senders.

DKIM

Search common selectors for published DKIM keys. A custom selector may be missed; this does not verify a message’s signature.

DMARC

Review the published policy. A monitoring policy, p=none, is different from quarantine or reject; it can satisfy provider minimum requirements.

TLS & STARTTLS

Probe the selected MX for STARTTLS support and inspect available certificate details. A blocked or timed-out probe is inconclusive.

IP reputation (RBL)

Check observed mail-server IPs against blocklists. These addresses may differ from your outbound sending infrastructure.

Reverse DNS (PTR)

Look up the reverse DNS record of the selected mail-server address.

The score

How the score is built

The configuration score summarizes the checks using fixed weights. Pass receives full weight, warning half, and fail zero. It is not a delivery probability or inbox-placement result.

Fixed weights

MX and DMARC receive higher weights in this configuration score. Review the individual findings alongside the total.

Current observations

The audit queries public DNS and probes accessible mail-server settings. Network restrictions or timeouts can limit the results.

Suggested next steps

Where the required values are known, results include example records. Confirm provider-specific values before changing DNS.

Read-only

The audit does not change your DNS, mailbox, or email provider settings.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need to change my DNS or install anything?

No. The audit reads public records and probes accessible mail-server settings. You or your provider applies any changes after reviewing the suggested next steps.

Does this prove my emails reach the inbox?

No. Authentication is one part of delivery. Sending reputation, recipient feedback, message content, and provider decisions also matter. This audit checks configuration; it does not send placement-test messages.

How does this compare with a message-based test?

Inboxproof checks a domain’s public configuration across several categories. A message-based test can inspect the actual message, signature, and alignment. The approaches provide different evidence.

What happens if I enter an email address?

Email is optional for an audit. Providing it saves your report and enables follow-up emails about your domain and monitoring options. Pro adds scheduled monitoring updates. See the privacy page for data handling.

What does the score actually mean?

It is a summary of the configuration checks using fixed weights: full weight for pass, half for warning, and zero for fail. A high score does not guarantee inbox delivery; a low score does not prove filtering.
